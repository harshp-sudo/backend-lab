Scaling & High Availability

A library can split newspapers into monthly boxes, assign books to different branches, keep copies, or appoint a replacement head clerk. These actions organize, distribute, duplicate and transfer authority in different ways. Naming them correctly prevents a bigger deployment from making a false promise.

Partitioning, sharding, replication and primary–replica operation solve related but distinct problems. Follow the limiting work or failure objective: useful data boundaries, independent ownership capacity, acknowledged/replayed copies, or safe takeover. Every additional mechanism needs explicit operations, routing and recovery rules.

Suggested reading guide

Start with Database Partitioning for local access and lifecycle organization, then Sharding for ownership across nodes. Ask whether the important invariant remains local and whether the proposed key distributes actual work rather than just row counts.

Read Replication for acknowledgment, visibility and loss/freshness guarantees. Continue to Master-Slave Architecture, which retains the requested historical title while using primary–replica terminology, for fencing, promotion, client reconnection and safe rejoin procedures.

Explore this branch

  • Database Partitioning

    Use meaningful boundaries for pruning and retention while respecting uniqueness, late arrivals and engine-specific maintenance locks.

  • Sharding

    Distribute ownership with a workload-aware key, then account for skew, routing, cross-owner invariants and verified live moves.

  • Replication

    Connect acknowledgment, durability, replay and read freshness to a tested failure envelope and independent historical recovery.

  • Master-Slave Architecture

    Study the primary–replica topology, current write authority, fencing, promotion, reconnects and safe rejoining of an old owner.
