Database Design & Management

A busy library needs a sensible catalog, useful indexes, enough branches/copies, and rules for safely changing its records. More shelves cannot fix a wrong catalog entry, and another copy does not automatically make every answer current. Database design is coordinating these different jobs around trustworthy facts.

This branch contains 15 complete lessons across four complementary categories. Each begins with an intuitive model, develops internal mechanics and trade-offs, then uses concrete failure stories and independently collapsible interview scenarios. PostgreSQL, MongoDB and PgBouncer examples identify their engine assumptions rather than claiming one product label provides every guarantee.

Suggested reading guide

Begin with Data Modeling & Schema Design: define facts, keys, relationships and the owner of each mutable value. Normalization and deliberate denormalization answer different questions about ownership and read work.

Then study Query Performance & Indexing with a real access pattern. Use execution and wait evidence before adding indexes or connections. Continue to Scaling & High Availability when a measured capacity or recovery need justifies additional boundaries/copies.

Use Consistency, Transactions & Operations to state what concurrent work promises and how live changes are rolled out. CAP consistency, ACID consistency, replica freshness and transaction isolation are distinct terms; connect them to one concrete operation instead of a feature checklist.

Explore this branch

  • Data Modeling & Schema Design

    Decide which facts belong together, how relationships are enforced and when duplicated or computed data has a justified maintenance contract.

  • Query Performance & Indexing

    Measure a real query or connection wait, choose a fitting access path, and control the write, storage and concurrency costs of improving reads.

  • Scaling & High Availability

    Distinguish local partition organization, distributed ownership, copied state and safe primary takeover instead of treating them as interchangeable scaling features.

  • Consistency, Transactions & Operations

    Define transaction and partition-time promises, protect concurrent decisions, and evolve the live database through compatible, verified steps.

After exploring, explain one concrete decision, its governing assumption and the failure that would change your answer. Opening an overview is navigation, not evidence of mastery; use the lesson recall cards and explicit completion control when ready.
