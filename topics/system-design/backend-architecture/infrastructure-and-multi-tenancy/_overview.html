Infrastructure & Multi-Tenancy

ELI5 big picture. An apartment complex needs a current directory, maintained shared roads, and private homes with clear keys and usage boundaries. A correct apartment label does not maintain the roads, and a guarded road does not authorize entry into every home. These lessons separate similarly distinct backend concerns.

This category covers locating changing service instances, managing service-to-service communication capabilities, and preserving customer contexts across shared resources. These are operational and ownership decisions with consequences for deployment, failures, costs, and isolation.

Why this branch exists. A discovery mechanism, mesh, and tenancy model are not interchangeable features. Each should solve a stated problem with known assumptions and operational ownership; adding more infrastructure without understanding its data path can make failures harder to diagnose.

Suggested reading guide

Start with Service Discovery to understand stable names, changing endpoints, readiness, selection, caches, and draining. Then study Service Mesh to see how communication policy and telemetry can be separated from application code, with their own control/data planes and operating costs.

Read Multi-Tenant Architecture when one solution serves distinct customer contexts. Follow tenant identity beyond the HTTP handler into data, caches, jobs, exports, and lifecycle operations. Cross-check the mesh lesson: workload authentication and encrypted communication do not replace customer/resource authorization.

Explore this branch

  • Service Discovery

    Find current service locations without hard-coding temporary machine addresses. Learn why readiness, cache freshness, long-lived connections, and graceful retirement matter as much as the first lookup.

  • Service Mesh

    Understand the managed infrastructure in a service communication path, including alternative proxy placement models. Evaluate capabilities, policy scope, retries, telemetry, and the cost of operating another subsystem.

  • Multi-Tenant Architecture

    Choose what customers share and what stays separate across data and runtime resources. Enforce trusted tenant context through asynchronous work and test isolation with overlapping resource identifiers.
