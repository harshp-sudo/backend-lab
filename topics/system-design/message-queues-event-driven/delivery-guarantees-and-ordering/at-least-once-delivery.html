At-Least-Once Delivery

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

A delivery driver leaves a parcel but loses the signed receipt. The depot sends another driver because it cannot tell whether delivery succeeded. Asking the depot to keep trying helps avoid a missing parcel, but the household must recognize a repeat if opening the parcel performs an expensive action.

At-least-once delivery retries eligible work when completion is uncertain, accepting possible duplicates. State the boundary: broker to consumer, application processing attempts, or committed business effects. A transport retry policy does not ensure that invalid work will eventually succeed, that expired data can be recovered, or that one financial effect will occur exactly once.

Imagine an order-paid message updating a local loyalty ledger. We want a committed loyalty credit before acknowledging the message. A crash between those two actions causes redelivery, so correctness requires a stable operation identity and atomic local effect/deduplication handling—not just an optimistic acknowledgment order.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

The redelivery path is expected recovery. Safety depends on the effect and duplicate record sharing a supported atomic boundary, with identity retained for the actual retry/replay horizon.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace the actual actor, communication, acknowledgment and recovery boundary.

1. Choose what is retried and for how long

In RabbitMQ 4.1 manual-acknowledgment consumption, an unacknowledged delivery can be requeued after channel/connection loss. In SQS, a message not successfully deleted can become visible again. Kafka consumption recovers from committed positions within retained history. These mechanisms differ, but all can repeat application work around failure.

Define the assumed retention, retry limits, broker durability/availability and eventual recovery conditions. At-least-once is not an unlimited guarantee against permanent outage, destructive administration or exhausted retention. A poison input needs an owned terminal/recovery policy rather than an infinite immediate retry loop.

2. Acknowledge after a durable meaningful result

For the loyalty example, validate the event, commit the local credit and only then acknowledge/advance progress. Acknowledging before the commit creates a loss window. Acknowledging afterward leaves a duplicate window if the acknowledgment or consumer response disappears.

Publisher confirms concern broker acceptance, while consumer acknowledgments concern that delivery. Neither automatically commits an external business system. Define what counts as completed before releasing transport responsibility; merely putting work in an untracked in-memory queue moves the loss window into your process.

3. Use stable business identity rather than each attempt’s ID

Carry a stable operation ID across producer retries, redeliveries and planned replay. Decide whether identity is an event occurrence, an order action or a provider operation, and include a namespace/tenant boundary where needed. Two legitimate credits must not accidentally share one deduplication key.

Store a payload fingerprint or equivalent conflict check when the same identity must imply the same request. A newly generated UUID on every retry defeats deduplication. Conversely, treating every message with the same customer ID as a duplicate would drop separate legitimate purchases.

4. Make local deduplication and the effect one transaction

A read-then-write duplicate check is racy: two workers can both find no record and both credit the account. Use a supported unique identity constraint and transaction so the duplicate marker and ledger change either commit together or roll back together. Define concurrent-conflict handling and the stored response/outcome.

Writing the marker first in a separate transaction can suppress an effect that never happened. Writing the credit first separately can double it after a crash. The exact SQL/isolation/error handling must fit the selected database; this lesson’s transaction trace is architectural, not a tested multi-database implementation.

5. Treat external effects as another boundary

A local transaction cannot atomically include an arbitrary email/payment provider. A durable outbox can preserve an effect intent alongside a local state change, but its relay can also repeat. Reuse a supported provider idempotency identity, query uncertain outcomes and reconcile before deciding whether to retry.

If the provider offers neither deduplication nor outcome discovery, an uncertain reply forces a business trade-off: repeat risk, omission risk or manual resolution. Do not claim a broker setting eliminates that uncertainty. A saga can coordinate compensations, but a compensation is a new action and may itself fail.

6. Operate replay, retries and deduplication retention together

Retain duplicate identities for the maximum supported late-delivery/replay horizon or use an authoritative invariant that still prevents repetition afterward. A ten-day replay can repeat a side effect if a seven-day dedup table is the only protection. Document how manual redrive behaves.

Use bounded retries/backoff for transient faults and an owned DLQ/quarantine path for poison inputs. Monitor delivery attempts, accepted identities, completed effects, duplicate conflicts and oldest unresolved age separately. A low queue depth after dropping failures does not prove the business backlog was resolved.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

Repeated delivery creates double points

Symptom: A timeout redelivery credits the same purchase twice.

Root Cause: The effect had no durable stable-identity protection.

Fix/Prevention: Coordinate the unique identity and local ledger effect atomically.

Dedup marker hides lost work

Symptom: A marker exists but no credit was committed.

Root Cause: Marker and effect were written in separate transactions.

Fix/Prevention: Make their commit/rollback boundary indivisible and reconcile existing inconsistencies.

Old replay repeats real effects

Symptom: A restored event charges again months later.

Root Cause: Deduplication expired before the supported replay horizon.

Fix/Prevention: Align retention/invariants and isolate rebuilds from live effect delivery.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: Expect retries around uncertainty, then protect each real effect with stable identity and an honest atomic boundary. Delivery attempts, acknowledged work and business completion are separate measurements. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

Why can acknowledgment after work create duplicates?

The effect can commit before a crash or lost acknowledgment causes the broker to repeat the uncertain delivery.

What identity must remain stable?

The intended business operation/event identity across attempts, with a scope that distinguishes legitimate separate actions.

Why is a duplicate lookup alone insufficient?

Concurrent workers and crashes can separate the check, marker and effect; they need a supported atomic uniqueness/transaction boundary.

Does at-least-once promise every business command eventually succeeds?

No. Validity, retention, retry policy, durable storage and eventual recovery assumptions bound what can be delivered and completed.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

A loyalty credit committed before the worker died. What does redelivery do?

The Scenario & Constraint

The broker did not observe acknowledgment and the same purchase returns. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Recognize the stable operation identity in the local atomic credit/dedup record, verify consistency and acknowledge without issuing another credit.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Identity storage costs space and needs lifecycle policy. A non-idempotent external effect needs a provider/reconciliation strategy beyond this local transaction.

Two workers both see no duplicate record. How do you prevent two effects?

The Scenario & Constraint

Visibility expiry or recovery overlap lets both process the same intent concurrently. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Use a unique scoped identity and a transaction coordinating that identity with the local effect, handling conflict/retry under the database contract.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Contention adds latency. A separate preflight lookup is useful for efficiency but cannot be the correctness boundary; a lease alone may expire while a worker still runs.

A month-old redrive passes a week-long dedup table. What changes?

The Scenario & Constraint

The operation must not be repeated even though replay beyond seven days is supported. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Align durable deduplication or a stronger domain invariant with the supported replay horizon, and gate old live-effect redrive through reconciliation.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Long retention has storage/privacy cost. Limiting replay is a viable alternative only if the product and recovery contract explicitly accept that limit.

A payment provider times out after receiving a request. Should you retry?

The Scenario & Constraint

A local consumer cannot tell whether the provider charged the card. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Preserve the intent, use the same supported provider operation identity, query/reconcile the uncertain result and retry only under that provider’s documented contract.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Without provider deduplication or outcome discovery, choose an explicit manual/omission/duplicate-risk policy. A local marker cannot prove a remote charge occurred.

A malformed event retries forever and blocks useful work. What changes?

The Scenario & Constraint

The decoder failure is deterministic and dependent ordering must be considered. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Classify the permanent error, quarantine with diagnostic context after bounded policy and assign repair/redrive ownership. Pause a dependent key if skipping breaks its invariant.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Quarantine can interrupt ordered progress and does not complete the business operation. Immediate retries help brief transient faults but only multiply this deterministic failure.

The listener acknowledges after handing work to a thread pool. Is that durable?

The Scenario & Constraint

The only copy of unfinished work is now in process memory. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Keep transport responsibility until a meaningful durable result or a supported durable handoff is committed; bound in-flight work and coordinate completion/progress.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Holding responsibility longer affects throughput/timeouts. A durable internal queue can be valid, but then its persistence and recovery become part of the end-to-end contract.
