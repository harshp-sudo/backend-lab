At-Most-Once Delivery

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

A receptionist crosses a reminder off the list before passing it to a runner. That prevents another runner taking the same list entry, but if the first runner drops it, nobody knows to try again. This can suit an expendable reminder. It is a poor way to handle the only instruction to return someone’s money.

At-most-once delivery or processing attempts intentionally avoid certain retries, accepting possible omission. Define the scope carefully: one consumption position may be attempted no more than once under a stated protocol, while a producer can still submit the same business intent twice as two distinct records. The label is not a universal duplicate-prevention property for an entire application.

Consider a Kafka 4.1 consumer sending an expendable live presence hint. One explicit design commits the next position successfully before attempting the hint. If the worker crashes afterward, recovery skips the hint. This sacrifices recovery deliberately; it is not a suitable accidental default for payments, inventory commands or audit records.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

The guarantee here is bounded to a named consumer/progress protocol. Omission is the deliberate trade-off; producer duplicates, manual rewinds and arbitrary external provider retries require separate contracts.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace the actual actor, communication, acknowledgment and recovery boundary.

1. Decide whether omission is acceptable

An old presence pulse can be superseded by the next pulse, so the product may accept losing one update. A refund command is not superseded by another unrelated refund and normally requires a recoverable outcome. Identify the business unit of loss and its maximum tolerable rate/age.

A best-effort design can remain observable: count accepted, skipped and attempted work, expose freshness and use an authoritative source for consequential decisions. Calling data telemetry or cache-like does not automatically make its loss acceptable; some monitoring or regulatory events are essential.

2. Implement an explicit progress-before-work example

The illustrative Kafka scope is 4.1 broker/client behavior with the Java 4.1.2 consumer API and an ordinary classic consumer group. Disable automatic offset commits for an explicit design, determine the next position correctly, confirm its commit and only then attempt the chosen expendable work.

A crash after that confirmed commit but before the effect means recovery does not redeliver that position. This is a conceptual trace, not deployed Java code or a benchmark. Do not generalize the example to all consumer libraries or confuse a consumer’s current read position with its committed recovery position.

3. Handle uncertain progress without inventing safety

If committing progress fails or its outcome/ownership is uncertain, do not blindly proceed with a nonrepeatable effect and assume the next owner will skip it. Stop the attempt and follow a supported recovery/ownership protocol before resuming. A replacement owner can otherwise attempt the same position.

Concurrent revocation, stale workers and manual resets complicate an end-to-end at-most-once attempt promise. State the assumed ownership and failure behavior. Broker progress alone cannot prevent an already running obsolete process from issuing an external effect; if that matters, enforce a stronger sink-side identity/fencing contract.

4. Avoid accidental batch-wide loss

If a batch contains ten records and progress is committed beyond all ten before processing any, a crash can omit the entire unfinished suffix. That may be acceptable for disposable samples, but the loss unit is now a batch rather than one hint. Larger batches improve amortization while enlarging that window.

Process a smaller unit or change the guarantee when the loss budget is exceeded. Auto-commit is not synonymous with a clean at-most-once contract: its timing relative to asynchronous work determines the actual failure windows. Trace the chosen library settings and application handoff instead of relying on the setting’s name.

5. Distinguish broker acknowledgment from upstream identity

RabbitMQ 4.1 automatic-acknowledgment mode considers delivery handled without waiting for the application’s durable effect, leaving a consumer-failure loss window. The exact delivery/queue/channel contract differs from Kafka’s position example. It still does not make independently republished business intents one operation.

Likewise, SQS Standard can deliver duplicate messages under its documented service semantics; simply deleting before work does not establish a universal no-duplicate end-to-end claim. Choose a verified mechanism and scope. When duplicate business effects are forbidden, use stable identity/atomic protection rather than relying on a transport label.

6. Recover expectations, not omitted work by magic

A no-retry policy cannot later promise that every omitted hint is reconstructed unless another source retained it. Use periodic fresh-state refresh, reconciliation against authority or explicit loss acceptance. Replaying history can be a new intentional processing attempt and must be governed separately.

Observe loss/freshness estimates, commit errors, consumer ownership events and upstream duplicate submissions. Test crash after confirmed progress and before work. If the test reveals unacceptable missing effects, switch to recoverable delivery plus safe repeated processing rather than obscuring the omission window with faster hardware.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

A refund vanishes after a crash

Symptom: Progress advanced but the refund never happened.

Root Cause: An expendable-work protocol was used for an authoritative command.

Fix/Prevention: Use recoverable responsibility and duplicate-safe effect handling.

Batch loss exceeds the budget

Symptom: Hundreds of samples disappear at once.

Root Cause: A large batch was committed before any work completed.

Fix/Prevention: Measure the batch loss window and reduce it or choose another guarantee.

Duplicates appear despite the label

Symptom: Two producer retries became two distinct records.

Root Cause: Consumer-position semantics were mistaken for business-intent identity.

Fix/Prevention: Use a stable upstream operation identity and the required sink protection.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: Use intentional omission only when the business can afford it, and state the protocol boundary. Avoiding a chosen retry path does not remove upstream duplicates or create durable business completion. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

What is the central loss window?

Progress/acknowledgment is made durable before the intended effect, and a crash between them causes recovery to skip unfinished work.

Can an at-most-once consumer position prevent producer duplicate intents?

No. Two distinct records may represent the same business intent unless identity is controlled separately.

Does auto-commit automatically define a safe guarantee?

No. Timing relative to processing, batching, ownership and asynchronous handoff determines the actual failure behavior.

What makes an appropriate expendable workload?

The product explicitly accepts omission, often because a later authoritative refresh supersedes intermediate hints, with a measured loss/freshness budget.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

A live presence hint is lost during a crash. Is retry required?

The Scenario & Constraint

Another fresh hint arrives shortly and no consequential decision relies solely on the missing pulse. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Accept the documented omission within its freshness budget, keep an authoritative refresh path and monitor loss/age. Retain a verified bounded at-most-once attempt protocol.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

No retry lowers recovery overhead but loses intermediate observations. If each pulse becomes billing/audit input, change to recoverable delivery and safe processing.

A refund is missing after an offset commit. How do you redesign?

The Scenario & Constraint

The consumer committed progress before calling the payment system and then crashed. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Reconcile the missing refund and replace this loss-prone protocol with durable responsibility, stable provider operation identity and explicit uncertain-outcome recovery.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Retries can duplicate effects without provider support. Faster processing narrows but never removes the crash gap; authoritative money movement cannot rely on that timing.

The progress commit times out. May the worker send the one-time effect?

The Scenario & Constraint

The application cannot establish commit success or current ownership. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Stop that effect attempt and resolve progress/ownership using the supported consumer protocol, or use a stronger sink-side identity contract before proceeding.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Stopping can omit work, which matches the chosen loss preference. Proceeding blindly can allow both this worker and a replacement to act; a timeout is not proof of rejection.

A single crash loses an entire batch. What do you change?

The Scenario & Constraint

The measured omission burst exceeds the product’s disposable-sample loss budget. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Reduce the precommitted unit and bound in-flight work, or adopt recoverable delivery if the required loss budget cannot be met by intentional omission.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Smaller commits reduce amortized throughput. No batch size makes the progress-before-effect crash window disappear.

Two identical notifications arrive despite at-most-once consumption. Why?

The Scenario & Constraint

A producer retried with a new record identity after an uncertain publish. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Trace both records and define stable business-intent identity at the producer/effect boundary when duplicate notification matters. Keep the consumer-position claim narrowly scoped.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Deduplication needs retention and payload-conflict policy. Two legitimate repeated notifications must not be collapsed just because their text matches.

RabbitMQ auto-ack loses a job when a worker dies. Is that a broker bug?

The Scenario & Constraint

The application acknowledged delivery automatically before completing durable work. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Recognize the configured loss window. Use manual acknowledgment after durable work with duplicate-safe effects when the job must be recoverable; bound in-flight work.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Manual acknowledgment adds redelivery/duplicate and prefetch responsibilities. Automatic acknowledgment can still fit explicitly expendable input, but does not make business completion durable.
