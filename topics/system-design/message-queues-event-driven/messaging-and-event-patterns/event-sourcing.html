Event Sourcing

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

Imagine keeping every deposit and withdrawal in a notebook instead of rubbing out yesterday’s balance. To know today’s balance, add the entries. A photograph of the balance can save time, but the notebook explains how you arrived there. Re-reading a withdrawal entry must never make the cash machine dispense the money again.

Event sourcing makes an ordered history of accepted domain events the authoritative record for a chosen business boundary. Current state is reconstructed by applying those events. It solves questions such as what happened, which rules accepted it, and how to rebuild another view. It also makes history format, concurrency, retention and replay long-lived responsibilities.

Consider an inventory reservation aggregate for one SKU at one warehouse. Commands ask to reserve or release stock; events record accepted facts. A normal database with current rows and an audit log may be simpler when the business does not need historical reconstruction. A retained Kafka topic alone does not establish an authoritative domain-event store with the concurrency and lifecycle guarantees this design needs.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

Only accepted commands create new facts. Replay reconstructs state from facts already accepted; it must not silently repeat the original real-world action.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace the actual actor, communication, acknowledgment and recovery boundary.

1. Separate intentions from facts

A reservation request is a command and can fail because available stock is insufficient. A StockReserved event records the accepted outcome and enough context for deterministic state reconstruction. Replaying that event applies the recorded change; it does not rerun today’s stock-acceptance rule and rewrite yesterday’s result.

Choose an aggregate boundary whose invariants can be protected by one supported append transaction. Keeping one stream per SKU/warehouse can serialize reservations there, but it does not atomically reserve an entire multi-warehouse order. Larger workflows need explicit coordination and compensation.

2. Protect concurrent appends with a real store contract

Load stream version 12, reconstruct state, validate the command, and attempt to append with expected version 12. If another writer won first, reject the conditional append, reload and re-evaluate the command. Blindly appending both writers’ decisions can oversell stock even though the final log contains both events.

This is a required capability to verify in the selected event store, not a claim that every broker supports expected-version append. The lesson uses a store-neutral model; no event-store SDK or deployed concurrency test is executed. A retry needs stable command identity as well as concurrency control if a previous append result is uncertain.

3. Reconstruct state and introduce trustworthy snapshots

A fold starts from an initial state and applies events in stream order. A snapshot stores reconstructed state together with the exact covered stream version and model version. Recovery loads that snapshot and applies only later events. Test that full replay and snapshot-plus-tail yield the same result.

Snapshots accelerate recovery; they should not silently replace authoritative history while the product still promises historical reconstruction. If events expire, disappear or become undecodable, a snapshot cannot necessarily answer older questions. Define backup, verification and data-lifecycle obligations before promising permanent replay.

4. Evolve events without changing their past meaning

Treat the stored event schema and the stream concurrency version as different concepts. A schema version describes how to interpret an event; a stream position protects ordering/concurrency. Use compatible readers or explicit upcasters that translate old representations for the current model.

Record decision-relevant historical values instead of consulting changing external services during replay. A shipping rule depending on yesterday’s exchange rate cannot be reliably reconstructed from today’s rate. Correct a business mistake with a new compensating/correcting fact when appropriate, preserving what happened and why rather than silently editing accepted history.

5. Rebuild projections without repeating side effects

A projection derives a read model from events. Give the rebuild an isolated destination/checkpoint and a deterministic handler, then catch up and switch readers with a defined handoff. Duplicated input and crash recovery still require safe projection updates and progress tracking.

Sending email or charging a card is an external effect, not a harmless fold operation. Keep replay paths from invoking live gateways. A durable effect-intent/outbox plus stable operation identity can coordinate live delivery; its provider boundary still needs reconciliation when the provider cannot deduplicate or reveal an uncertain result.

6. Operate the history as a long-lived product

Monitor append conflicts, corrupt/unknown events, reconstruction time, projection lag and failed live effects separately. Exercise old event fixtures, snapshot version mismatches, duplicate command retries and rebuild catch-up before depending on disaster recovery.

An append-only design does not erase privacy, correction or retention obligations. Decide what personal data belongs outside the immutable business history and obtain applicable policy guidance. An audit requirement can justify the cost; an ordinary CRUD screen often cannot. The conceptual diagram and failure traces here do not benchmark a specific event store.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

Rebuild sends real notifications

Symptom: A replay emails every past customer.

Root Cause: The state fold invokes a live external gateway.

Fix/Prevention: Isolate replay and represent live effects through an explicit recoverable delivery path.

Two reservations oversell stock

Symptom: Concurrent commands both saw the last available unit.

Root Cause: Appending events did not protect the expected aggregate version.

Fix/Prevention: Require conditional append and re-evaluate conflicting commands.

Old events become unreadable

Symptom: Recovery fails after a schema deployment.

Root Cause: Only current payloads were tested and history readers were removed.

Fix/Prevention: Keep historical fixtures, compatible readers/upcasters and verified snapshot-plus-tail recovery.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: Make accepted history authoritative only when its business value pays for explicit concurrency, durable evolution and safe reconstruction. Replaying a fact must never quietly repeat its external effect. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

What makes this event sourcing rather than merely publishing events?

Accepted domain-event history is authoritative for reconstructing the chosen boundary, with an explicit concurrency and lifecycle contract.

What must accompany a snapshot?

The exact covered stream position/model version and a verified method to apply the remaining tail.

How do schema and stream versions differ?

Schema versions describe representation; stream versions describe ordered history and can protect conditional append.

Why must replay avoid live effects?

It revisits already accepted facts; calling external gateways again can repeat irreversible actions.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

Two last-unit reservations arrive together. How do you prevent overselling?

The Scenario & Constraint

Both commands read the same stream version and each believes one unit remains. No cross-aggregate transaction is required. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Use an event store with atomic expected-version append. Let one append succeed; reload and revalidate the loser, rejecting it if stock is gone. Preserve command identity across uncertain retries.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Conflicts add retries and latency. A row-based conditional transaction may be simpler; separate streams cannot protect a shared stock invariant merely because their events are durable.

A rebuild is about to resend a million receipts. How do you make it safe?

The Scenario & Constraint

Historical events must rebuild a projection, while only newly accepted orders should trigger live receipts. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Run the projection in an isolated replay path without live gateways, catch up using explicit checkpoints and keep live delivery intents separate with duplicate-safe identity.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Isolation and handoff add operational work. Simply checking wall-clock age is unsafe for delayed legitimate events; decide effect ownership explicitly.

Snapshot recovery disagrees with a full replay. Which result do you trust?

The Scenario & Constraint

A model change shipped and the snapshot covers an uncertain event position. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Stop promoting that snapshot, compare historical fixtures and regenerate from validated history with a covered position and model version. Verify snapshot-plus-tail equivalence before reuse.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Full recovery costs time and requires retained readable history. If the log was discarded, reconstruction may be impossible; do not claim the snapshot is merely a disposable optimization then.

Replaying old orders changes their calculated totals. What is missing?

The Scenario & Constraint

The handler consults today’s exchange-rate service but must reproduce the accepted historical amount. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Use recorded decision-relevant historical values or a versioned historical source and make reconstruction deterministic. Correct future logic without silently changing accepted facts.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Richer events require careful data ownership and lifecycle policy. Re-running current business rules can be a separate simulation, but must not masquerade as historical reconstruction.

An accepted quantity was wrong. Do you edit the historical event?

The Scenario & Constraint

The business needs an explainable correction and existing projections have already processed the fact. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Record a supported correcting/compensating domain fact with causation and rebuild/update projections consistently. Investigate the command-validation defect that admitted the original.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Correction semantics add domain complexity. A regulated deletion requirement may require a deliberately different storage/redaction design; append-only rhetoric does not settle that policy.

A small catalog CRUD service is slow to build. Does it need event sourcing?

The Scenario & Constraint

Only current product descriptions and basic audit entries are required; no historical replay feature is funded. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Prefer ordinary transactional current-state storage with an appropriate audit trail, documenting the requirements that would justify event sourcing later.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Future reconstruction may need redesign. Event sourcing can fit a history-centered domain, but adopting compatibility/replay infrastructure without that need creates unnecessary delivery and operating cost.
