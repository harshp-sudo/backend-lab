Publish-Subscribe Pattern

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

A school sends one announcement to the sports office and the music office. Each office keeps its own inbox and may have several helpers sharing its work. If all helpers share one single inbox instead, the music office might take the only slip before sports sees it. Copies for departments and workers sharing a copy solve different problems.

Publish-subscribe distributes a publication to independent interested subscriptions through a routing mechanism. A competing-consumer queue distributes work within one consumption role. A design can combine both: one subscription per application, several workers per subscription. The publisher avoids addressing every worker, but event meaning, schema and recovery contracts still connect the teams.

Use an account-status event consumed by billing notifications and risk analysis. Both applications need their own reaction/progress, while each can scale its internal workers. State whether a subscription is durable, which events it receives, what happens while it is absent, and whether historical replay is supported. A topic name alone answers none of those questions.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

The fan-out boundary supplies independent subscription progress; the worker-sharing boundary distributes one application’s work. Verify both against the selected broker.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace the actual actor, communication, acknowledgment and recovery boundary.

1. Define the fact and interested roles

An account event must state a meaningful owned fact, not silently command every subscriber to perform a coordinated transaction. Identify which applications require the event and which reactions are optional. Independent subscribers may succeed at different times; publication is not proof that all reactions finished.

Version the event contract and include stable identity/subject information appropriate to the consumers. Producers should not encode worker addresses into every event, but consumers still depend on semantics and compatibility. The existing Event-Driven Architecture lesson explains that larger responsibility boundary.

2. Create independent subscription state

Give notifications and risk distinct subscription/queue/group progress according to the actual product model. Sharing one work queue/group between them can distribute events between applications rather than delivering each application its required input. Inside notifications, competing workers can share that application’s subscription.

RabbitMQ’s fan-out tutorial illustrates separate bound queues, while a retained-log system can expose independent application groups. These are concrete examples of distribution, not identical retention/durability implementations. Name how each subscription is provisioned, who owns it and which publisher routes to it.

3. Choose durability, filtering and late-join behavior

An ephemeral online subscription can miss publications while disconnected; a durable subscription may retain eligible work under its documented retention/limits. A late subscriber may need a separate bootstrap or retained replay, because ordinary future-only publication cannot reconstruct facts it never received.

Filters reduce traffic but become part of the subscription’s data contract. If a filter changes, older excluded events do not necessarily reappear. Validate whether filtering operates on headers, attributes or payload and whether the event contains enough context to evaluate the intended rule safely.

4. Isolate slow or failed subscribers

Risk analysis may be temporarily slow while notifications remain healthy. Independent queues/positions allow separate catch-up, but the broker and storage are still finite. Define per-subscription backlog, retention, admission and failure policy so one lagging consumer cannot consume all shared capacity.

Do not let the publisher synchronously wait for every application’s business effect unless that coordination is explicitly required. If billing completion depends on risk approval, model a workflow/command contract rather than hiding a mandatory dependency behind a loosely named event.

5. Handle duplicate publication and partial observations

A publication or acknowledgment reply can be lost, so producer/subscriber recovery needs stable identity and duplicate-safe effects where required. A subscriber’s acknowledgment concerns its own work. It does not acknowledge another independent application’s result or make the whole fan-out an atomic business transaction.

Verify the broker’s routing/acceptance/failure guarantees instead of assuming all subscriptions receive atomically forever. An application that requires cross-subscriber agreement needs a separate supported coordination or reconciliation design, with honest pending/failure states.

6. Evolve and operate the event contract

Test new fields/types, removed subscribers, recreated durable state, changed filters and replay after an outage. Add compatibility checks and an ownership path for consumers falling behind. Additive schema changes can still break strict decoders; verify actual reader behavior.

Observe publication/route failures, eligible versus filtered events, lag/age per subscription and completed business effects. No live broker is executed in the topology diagram. The point is to choose independence deliberately while retaining enough coordination to meet the real business promise.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

One department misses half the facts

Symptom: Risk and notifications each see different events.

Root Cause: They share one competing consumption identity.

Fix/Prevention: Create independent application subscriptions, then scale workers inside each.

New subscriber assumes full history

Symptom: A newly launched service has an incomplete account view.

Root Cause: Future-only pub/sub was treated as replayable authoritative history.

Fix/Prevention: Provide a matching bootstrap/replay source or revise the view contract.

Slow listener fills shared storage

Symptom: A failing optional consumer hurts essential publication.

Root Cause: Subscriber isolation ignored broker-wide finite resources.

Fix/Prevention: Bound backlog/retention and define explicit overload and recovery behavior.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: Design independent subscription contracts and worker sharing separately, with explicit durability, eligibility, catch-up and business coordination requirements. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

What separates pub/sub from competing workers?

Independent application subscriptions need their own input/progress, while workers in one role divide that role’s work.

Does a topic imply durable replay?

No. Subscription storage, retention and replay are product/configuration contracts.

What does changing a filter not automatically do?

It does not necessarily backfill historical events that the earlier subscription excluded.

Does one publication mean every subscriber completed its effect?

No. Each application has independent delivery, processing and outcome boundaries.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

Risk and notifications share one queue and miss events. What changes?

The Scenario & Constraint

Both applications require every eligible account fact, but their workers compete for one delivery stream. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Create independent application subscriptions under the selected broker, then let interchangeable workers share work within each application. Plan bootstrap for facts already missed.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Separate subscriptions add retained state and monitoring. If only one of the applications should act on each task, competing delivery may have been the right contract; clarify the role first.

Risk is a day behind. Must notifications stop?

The Scenario & Constraint

Risk is independently recoverable and notifications are required to remain available. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Isolate subscription progress/resources, bound risk backlog/retention and control its catch-up load while notifications continue under their own guarantee.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Storage/quotas cost resources and eventual retention exhaustion needs an explicit fallback. Shared broker failure can still affect both; independent subscriptions are not unlimited physical isolation.

A new subscriber needs last month’s account history. Is subscribing enough?

The Scenario & Constraint

The current channel promises only future publications and has no retained replay. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Bootstrap from a supported authoritative snapshot/history with a defined handoff to live events, or choose retained history before promising completeness.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Bootstrap adds reconciliation and source load. An online-only listener can accept missing history, but that is a different product view.

A new filter should include past premium accounts. What is missing?

The Scenario & Constraint

Earlier events were excluded and the new consumer expects a complete derived view. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Determine whether historical replay exists and run an explicit bounded backfill/bootstrap with stable event/projection identity and a coordinated live catch-up position.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Backfills consume capacity and may need a second view generation. Merely editing the filter changes future eligibility, not necessarily already discarded history.

An event secretly requires risk approval before billing success. What do you change?

The Scenario & Constraint

The publisher claims final completion before the required subscriber has responded. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Model the mandatory approval as an explicit workflow/decision boundary with durable pending/final states and recovery ownership. Keep optional notifications independently subscribed.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Explicit coordination adds latency/coupling, but the dependency already exists in business semantics. Calling the message an event does not make the requirement optional.

A producer adds a field and one subscriber breaks. What now?

The Scenario & Constraint

That subscriber uses a strict decoder while others accept the new payload. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Check the actual schema/reader compatibility contract, deploy a compatible versioning transition and recover failed deliveries with stable identity after repair.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Compatibility support and parallel versions add work. Assuming every additive change is safe ignores real decoder behavior; dropping the subscriber silently may violate a required reaction.
