Write-Through Cache

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

At a hotel desk, the clerk updates the official reservation book while you wait, then updates the convenient display. You should not be told the reservation is complete merely because the display changed. Write-through caching puts the authoritative write on the synchronous path, but two places can still disagree if something fails between their updates.

In a write-through abstraction, writes invoke the configured authoritative writer synchronously instead of merely queueing them for later. Ehcache 3.11 CacheLoaderWriter is a concrete provider reference. Exact call ordering, exceptions and cache visibility must be checked for the chosen implementation; the name alone does not define a distributed transaction.

Our teaching model commits a versioned product update to the origin, then publishes a cache view and acknowledges according to a documented outcome policy. It deliberately separates durable business success from cache synchronization status. Other providers may expose a different sequence, which must be stated rather than silently substituted.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

This is the explicitly chosen teaching sequence, not an assertion about every provider’s internals. A timeout or failure at any arrow needs an outcome and recovery policy.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace the copy, authoritative owner, acknowledgment and recovery boundary.

1. Choose the write owner and public acknowledgment

Give mutations one authoritative domain owner. Identify what completion means: the origin committed, cache publication completed, or a caller-visible receipt says the committed change is awaiting cache repair. Do not let a generic cache facade invent business success semantics.

Ehcache 3.11 delegates writes through CacheLoaderWriter; synchronous invocation is the relevant contrast with deferred write-behind. A production integration still has to define validation, authorization, transaction boundaries and recovery inside/around the writer.

2. Validate and commit where the invariant lives

Authenticate/authorize the action and enforce constraints in the authoritative write boundary. Use expected versions, conditional updates or appropriate transactions for concurrent changes. A cache-only existence/stock check cannot reserve a database fact against other writers.

If the origin rejects the write before committing, do not publish the proposed value as accepted cached truth. If a timeout leaves the origin outcome unknown, query/retry under a safe operation identity contract. “Writer threw” does not always mean “nothing changed.”

3. Publish or invalidate under a race-aware policy

After the origin commits in this model, update or invalidate a derived cache view with its version/freshness policy. Partial failures must preserve the truthful origin outcome.

Publishing a newer value can still race an older in-flight loader. A guarded/version-aware fill or a suitable bypass may be needed for strict readers; simply writing the cache last does not prove fleet-wide freshness.

4. Define retry and repair ownership

Record enough durable information to recover committed operations whose cache update failed, if the freshness promise requires it. Use a publication/invalidation intent coupled to the authoritative write where appropriate, and make repair version-aware so delayed work cannot overwrite newer state.

Retrying a non-idempotent business write because only cache publication failed can repeat an effect. Separate business operation retry from optional cache repair. A returned version/operation receipt can let read-your-write clients use an authoritative or minimum-version path while other views converge.

5. Budget latency and all readers

Synchronous origin work is on the caller’s critical path; cache or writer delays add to it according to the actual sequence. Set deadlines, bound concurrency and observe origin commit latency, cache publication, partial failures and repair backlog separately.

All write paths must participate in the policy or emit changes for repair. A batch job writing the database directly can stale a cache even when ordinary API writes are write-through. Local process copies, replicas and browser responses also need their own freshness rules. Test concurrent loading, direct writes, failures between steps and lost responses.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

Cache success is called durable success

Symptom: A displayed update disappears after origin failure.

Root Cause: The design acknowledged a cache-only publication.

Fix/Prevention: Acknowledge the real authoritative boundary and keep uncommitted proposals separate.

A cache timeout repeats the business effect

Symptom: A second mutation runs after the first origin commit.

Root Cause: Repair and business retry shared one blind retry loop.

Fix/Prevention: Reconcile the effect and retry only the missing derived-state work.

Every reader is assumed current

Symptom: A local worker or old loader returns stale data.

Root Cause: Synchronous writing was mistaken for all-tier consistency.

Fix/Prevention: Trace each reader/tier and apply versions, invalidation or bypass as required.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: Write-through places authoritative work on the synchronous path; define acknowledgment, partial failure and reader freshness explicitly instead of treating two storage actions as one commit. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

What is synchronous in write-through?

The configured authoritative writer is invoked on the write path rather than merely queueing its work. Exact provider order and acknowledgment still need definition.

Does the pattern make SQL and cache one atomic commit?

No. State the true transaction boundary and handle partial success or uncertainty across the separate actions.

Why separate cache repair from business retry?

The business change may already be committed. Repeating it can duplicate effects while the actual missing work is only cache synchronization.

Does a write-through update guarantee every cache tier is fresh?

No. Old fills, other workers, browser/edge copies and bypass writers need their own coherence policy.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

The origin committed but cache publication timed out. What does the caller learn?

The Scenario & Constraint

The writer sequence is origin-first. A product update is durable, but the cache may still hold version 1. What will you do?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Preserve the committed operation result, record/execute cache repair under a defined policy and provide a receipt/version or truthful partial-status contract. Route strict read-your-write access to an owner capable of the committed version. Do not claim the database rolled back because cache synchronization failed.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Repair and receipts add state; synchronous cache-independent authoritative reads may be simpler for strict paths. A timeout may mean the cache action succeeded but its reply was lost. Retrying the whole non-idempotent mutation is not equivalent to repairing a copy.

A cache accepts a value and the origin rejects it. Can that be a successful save?

The Scenario & Constraint

An implementation publishes before validating/committing the authoritative change, then replies success. What will you change?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Define success around the authoritative operation and prevent uncommitted proposed values from being exposed as accepted truth. Align actual sequence/provider semantics, clean up divergent entries and test origin validation failures. Keep authorization and concurrency rules in the domain owner.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

A separate pending/proposed state can fit a workflow but must be visible as such. Delayed write-behind is another contract, not a justification for describing a cache-only acknowledgment as synchronous durable success.

A write-through cache publishes version 2, then a loader writes version 1. What prevents it?

The Scenario & Constraint

A miss reader began before the commit and completes after cache publication. The editing user needs the acknowledged version. What will you do?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Use version-aware/guarded publication or immutable version-addressed values with trustworthy current-version discovery, and provide an authoritative/minimum-version read where required. Test the exact interleaving rather than assuming synchronous writes serialize every reader.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Version metadata and checks cost coordination; bounded stale display is simpler under a different promise. A guard that disappears on eviction/failover cannot silently accept obsolete data. Write-through naming alone does not establish linearizable reads.

An overnight database job bypasses the writer and readers stay stale. What is the ownership fix?

The Scenario & Constraint

Normal API writes follow the facade; maintenance and imports directly alter the same records. What will you do?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Route changes through one policy owner or publish durable change/invalidation information for every write path. Inventory bypass writers, define version/repair behavior and reconcile existing stale copies. Keep the authoritative database operation valid even when optional cache repair is delayed.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

A central write service can reduce flexibility; change capture/outbox-style publication adds operational machinery. Assuming “all writes use the cache” without auditing administrative paths leaves the same consistency gap.

Write latency doubles after introducing write-through. Is write-behind a free optimization?

The Scenario & Constraint

The product requires committed inventory changes before confirming a purchase. What will you investigate?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Measure origin, cache and queue/connection times, optimize the actual synchronous work and verify the required acknowledgment boundary. Keep correctness-critical writes authoritative; consider asynchronous derived-cache repair where policy allows instead of silently deferring the inventory commit.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Asynchronous work can reduce response delay but changes recovery/durability obligations. Write-behind may suit replaceable telemetry under a different requirement, not automatically a confirmed stock promise. More concurrency can worsen a saturated origin.

The writer times out after sending a non-repeatable update. Should it retry from scratch?

The Scenario & Constraint

The origin may have committed, and the facade cannot tell whether the lost reply represented success. What will you do?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Reuse a stable operation identity/version and reconcile the authoritative result before repeating the business effect. Keep bounded retries and cache repair separate, and return a truthful unresolved/pending state when the contract cannot establish completion immediately.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Durable operation outcomes add retention/recovery work; naturally idempotent set-to-state operations can simplify some cases. A thrown loader/writer exception is not a transaction rollback certificate across network boundaries.
