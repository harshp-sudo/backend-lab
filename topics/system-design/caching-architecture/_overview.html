Caching Architecture

A study desk keeps frequently used books close, while the library remains the source of the collection. The desk saves trips, but a handwritten note can become outdated and the desk can fill up. Caching architecture decides which copies are useful, how they stay acceptable and what happens when everyone needs the library at once.

This branch follows a copy from its first miss through reuse, change, removal and failure. First identify the authoritative owner and allowed freshness. Then choose a placement/product and an explicit read/write contract, before scaling distribution. Faster access is useful only when the returned answer and failure behavior fit the task.

Suggested reading guide

Begin with Caching Foundations & Technologies to decide whether caching helps and where its copies belong. Redis and Memcached are concrete technologies with explicit versions and deployment assumptions.

Use Read & Write Caching Patterns to trace who loads, writes and acknowledges work. Continue to Lifecycle, Eviction & Consistency to reason about stale copies and concurrent changes.

Finish with Distributed Caching & Edge Scaling for placement, HTTP edge policy and bounded origin recovery. A cache outage or cold start is part of the design, not an exceptional detail.
