Distributed Tracing

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

A parcel gets a tracking number as it travels through sorting offices. Each office records its own handling step and which step led to it. That history helps explain a late delivery. Missing scans do not prove the parcel skipped an office, and adding every overlapping handling time would exaggerate the journey.

Distributed tracing relates operations across service and asynchronous boundaries so one user journey can be investigated. A trace contains spans describing bounded operations, with identifiers, relationships, timing and selected attributes. It complements aggregate metrics and discrete logs; its sampled, instrumented view is not a complete record of all business activity. Follow checkout calling inventory and pricing in parallel, then queueing fulfillment after the response.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

The diagram shows causal relationships, not a promise that every operation is recorded or that parallel durations add to end-to-end latency.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace ownership, state transitions, decision evidence and the failure/recovery boundary.

1. Define span boundaries around meaningful work

A span describes one operation with its own span ID, trace identity, time information, kind/status and selected attributes/events. A parent represents a particular causal relationship, not mere timestamp proximity. Name operations with bounded meaningful values rather than embedding every customer URL. Instrument wait and remote calls where useful, but avoid thousands of tiny spans that obscure the question and increase cost.

2. Carry context explicitly across boundaries

W3C Trace Context standardizes traceparent/tracestate for supported HTTP propagation; libraries inject/extract context according to their configured propagator. In-process asynchronous tasks and messaging need correct context capture/activation too. Inspect each hop: producer injection, intermediary forwarding, consumer extraction and new span creation. Headers establish correlation, not caller authentication or permission. Validate untrusted input and apply privacy/trust policy without placing secrets in trace fields.

3. Model queued, batched and retried work honestly

A span has at most one parent but can link to multiple span contexts. A batch combining many independently produced messages cannot truthfully have all of them as direct parents; links can retain those causal associations. Whether a later consumer continues a trace or starts a linked trace depends on the instrumentation and messaging semantic convention. Represent individual attempts and the logical operation distinctly when retries matter, so repeated work is visible rather than silently replacing a previous attempt.

4. Interpret the critical path and clocks

Suppose checkout takes 140 ms: inventory and pricing overlap for 80 ms and 100 ms, followed by 40 ms of serial processing. Their combined contribution is 100+40=140 ms under this simplified full-overlap example, not 80+100+40. Exclusive/self time and wait interpretation depend on complete boundaries and backend behavior. Cross-machine wall clocks can be skewed; do not infer impossible causality from raw aligned timestamps alone. Compare relationships and locally measured durations and investigate synchronization/visualization assumptions.

5. State what sampling and export can hide

Head sampling chooses before knowing the complete outcome, so it cannot guarantee retention of all eventual errors. Tail sampling evaluates assembled later information but needs buffering, trace affinity and time bounds; earlier dropped spans cannot be recreated. Partial traces can also result from missing instrumentation, propagation breaks, exporter loss or late spans. Monitor collection health and use metrics to estimate population-level rates, rather than extrapolating biased retained error traces as if they were every request. Restrict sensitive attributes and retention across the whole path.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

Trace breaks at a queue

Symptom: Consumer work appears unrelated to checkout.

Root Cause: Context is not injected/extracted or activated at the message boundary.

Fix/Prevention: Inspect each carrier hop and use correct messaging instrumentation and relationships.

Parallel spans summed

Symptom: The chart appears to explain more time than the request took.

Root Cause: Overlapping work is added as if serial.

Fix/Prevention: Analyze critical-path overlap and explicit wait/processing boundaries.

All retained traces fail

Symptom: The service is declared 100% broken.

Root Cause: Outcome-biased sampling is mistaken for a complete population.

Fix/Prevention: Use unsampled aggregate outcome metrics and inspect the retention policy.

What does context propagation preserve?

It carries correlation information so downstream spans can establish the intended relationship. It does not automatically record spans or authorize the caller.

When are links useful beyond a parent?

For multiple causal inputs, batches, fan-in or intentionally separate traces, where one parent cannot represent the relationship honestly.

How do 80 ms and 100 ms fully overlapping calls plus 40 ms serial work contribute?

The simplified critical-path duration is max(80,100)+40=140 ms. Summing all work gives resource activity, not elapsed request time.

Why does a missing error trace not disprove an error?

Sampling, dropped spans, missing instrumentation, propagation and export failures can all omit relevant evidence.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

HTTP spans connect but queued fulfillment has a new unrelated trace. Where do you inspect?

The Scenario & Constraint

Messages cross a queue and a consumer starts asynchronous work after receiving them. What will you do?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Check supported carrier injection, intermediary preservation, extraction and active context at span creation. Use parent/link relationships consistent with the selected messaging instrumentation and intended lifetime.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Copying a trace ID string alone does not establish correct span parentage. Reusing one context across unrelated messages can incorrectly merge work, and sampling/export gaps can still produce partial traces.

One consumer batch contains fifty independently produced messages. Which one is the parent?

The Scenario & Constraint

The batch has multiple causal inputs and the backend supports links. What representation fits?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Represent the batch operation with appropriate links to input contexts and choose any parent only when it reflects a genuine single upstream operation. Preserve per-message processing evidence where needed by the chosen convention.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

A span cannot simply acquire fifty parents. Links add lookup/visualization complexity and backend support varies; treating the first message as the sole origin can hide the rest.

A 1% head sampler misses the one payment failure you need. What policy changes?

The Scenario & Constraint

Operations wants every future failed request trace retained while controlling telemetry volume. What trade-off fits?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Consider outcome-aware tail retention with adequate collection, trace routing and bounded wait resources, plus targeted instrumentation and reliable aggregate metrics. State that any prior head drop or pipeline loss cannot be undone downstream.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Tail selection costs memory/state and cannot guarantee unseen or late outcomes. Raising head sampling may help but does not establish perfect error retention unless the complete collection/delivery path meets that stronger contract.

A child appears to start before its remote parent. Is causal order broken?

The Scenario & Constraint

Services use clocks with measurable skew and the backend draws a waterfall from wall timestamps. What investigation fits?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Check actual trace relationships, local durations, clock synchronization and backend correction assumptions. Use known send/receive boundaries to distinguish skew from instrumentation errors before blaming the business protocol.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Sorting wall timestamps is not a distributed ordering protocol. Clock corrections can improve display but do not repair missing spans or establish causality where instrumentation never recorded it.

Two parallel calls total 180 ms but the parent lasts 140 ms. What conclusion fits?

The Scenario & Constraint

Calls of 80 ms and 100 ms overlap, with 40 ms serial work after them. What will you explain?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Under the stated full-overlap assumptions, elapsed contribution is 100+40=140 ms; analyze the critical path and waits instead of summing child durations. Compare actual start/end evidence before applying the simplified arithmetic.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Concurrency can still consume more aggregate resource time than elapsed time. Missing spans, nested children or incomplete overlap alter the interpretation, so a waterfall is evidence to inspect rather than a universal additive formula.

A client supplies trace headers and sensitive baggage. Can services trust them for tenant access?

The Scenario & Constraint

An external caller chooses correlation values and attempts to influence downstream behavior. What boundary fits?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Keep authentication/authorization independent, validate and bound propagated context, and apply a deliberate privacy/trust policy at ingress. Avoid storing secrets or personal data in tracing fields and restrict backend access.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Discarding all context can reduce cross-system diagnosis, but accepting it as authority is unsafe. A sampled flag is not permission for unbounded telemetry resource use or privileged data access.
