Blue-Green Deployment

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

Imagine a restaurant building a second, fully prepared kitchen next door. Customers keep eating food from the blue kitchen while the green one is checked. The host then directs new orders to green. Switching the host back is quick only if blue still understands the orders and ingredients that changed meanwhile.

Blue-green deployment keeps two release environments and moves serving traffic between them. Its purpose is to prepare and verify a replacement before broad exposure, reducing the time the live environment spends being modified. It is a traffic and environment strategy, not a promise that every session, message and database write can be undone. Follow a checkout service whose new release changes how addresses are stored.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

Read the serving path separately from the persistent-state path: returning traffic does not reverse completed writes.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace ownership, state transitions, decision evidence and the failure/recovery boundary.

1. Define what the two colors contain

List the application, configuration, network routes, workers and dependencies that belong to each environment. Identical names or VM sizes do not guarantee equivalent permissions, certificates or quotas. Keep a release identity and a configuration inventory so a successful green check means something reproducible. Decide whether the database is shared, replicated or migrated separately; that choice dominates recovery risk.

2. Prepare green without activating every side effect

Deploy the tested artifact to green and check real dependency reachability using safe test data. Suppress duplicate cron jobs, event consumers and external notifications until their ownership is deliberately transferred. Two running environments must not charge the same order twice. If shadow requests are used, ensure writes and confidential data are handled explicitly rather than assuming mirrored requests are harmless.

3. Expand the address schema before switching

For this hypothetical checkout change, add an optional structured-address representation while preserving the old fields. New and old code must tolerate the overlap. Backfill and verify data separately, then move readers and writers. Remove the old representation only after its users and the rollback window are gone. Compatibility can cost extra storage and implementation work but prevents a fast traffic reversal from becoming a new outage.

4. Cut over through a known routing mechanism

A load balancer target change, proxy route or DNS update has different propagation and connection behavior. Measure actual traffic by serving release, not just the control-plane update response. Existing keep-alive connections or WebSockets may remain on blue while new connections reach green. Coordinate draining, session storage and timeout policy so the transition preserves the service contract.

5. Observe and recover within a bounded window

Watch user success, latency and business correctness by version, plus shared dependencies. Suppose green is healthy for new sessions but old carts fail: pause exposure and inspect session/schema compatibility before switching everything back. Retain blue capacity and required credentials until recovery criteria expire. After retirement, recovery is a new provisioning action with its own time and cost, not an instantaneous switch.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

Two schedulers own the same work

Symptom: Invoices are generated twice after green starts.

Root Cause: Background jobs activate independently in both environments.

Fix/Prevention: Assign job ownership and idempotency explicitly; validate non-HTTP paths before cutover.

The switch is not universal

Symptom: Some users stay on the retired version.

Root Cause: Long-lived connections, DNS caches or session affinity outlast the route edit.

Fix/Prevention: Measure version traffic, drain deliberately and retain capacity for a bounded overlap.

Fast route reversal, broken data

Symptom: Blue crashes after rollback.

Root Cause: Green wrote a format blue cannot read.

Fix/Prevention: Preserve compatibility or plan a separate verified forward/data recovery path.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: Explain how preparing a second environment shortens the serving transition while data compatibility, background work and connection lifetimes still govern recovery. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

What do blue and green represent?

Two release environments that can coexist while traffic ownership changes. Color names are labels; they do not establish data isolation or equivalence.

Why can routing rollback succeed while recovery fails?

Persisted writes, sessions and messages survive the route change. The former application may no longer understand that state.

What must happen to old connections?

Use a deliberate drain/timeout strategy and measure remaining traffic. Existing connections are not necessarily moved by a new-connection routing change.

Why does retirement need an explicit window?

Keeping old capacity costs money, but removing it ends the quick reversal option. Base retirement on compatibility, observed health and recovery requirements.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

Green renamed a database column; blue fails after traffic reversal. What now?

The Scenario & Constraint

Checkout errors rise after cutover. The old image expects the removed address field, and green has already accepted orders. What recovery will you choose?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Establish the current data shape and protect accepted orders. Restore a compatible read/write path through a forward fix or a verified compatibility migration; use blue only if it can safely consume current state. Adopt expand/migrate/contract for the next change.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Restoring a pre-release backup may erase legitimate later orders and needs reconciliation. Keeping both formats costs complexity. A routing reversal is useful only inside its actual data-compatibility boundary.

Ten percent of users still hit blue after cutover. What will you inspect?

The Scenario & Constraint

The route control panel shows green at 100%, but existing carts continue reaching blue. There is no permission to discard active sessions. What do you do?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Compare connection age, affinity, cache/DNS behavior and serving-version telemetry. Keep compatible blue capacity while draining according to a tested session contract, and verify new versus existing connections separately.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Forcing disconnects may simplify retirement but violate user continuity. Waiting indefinitely wastes capacity. Changing DNS does not force every client or open connection to refresh immediately.

Both colors process scheduled refunds. How do you prevent double payment?

The Scenario & Constraint

Green validation starts its background scheduler while blue is still live. The payment provider may accept repeated requests. What boundary is missing?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Separate validation from activation of side-effecting jobs, establish one logical owner for the work, and make payment effects idempotent with durable business identifiers. Reconcile any possibly duplicated refunds before resuming.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

A routing switch affects HTTP ingress, not independently running workers. Singleton ownership alone cannot erase ambiguous external effects after a crash; durable outcome checks are still needed.

Green passes probes but collapses at full traffic. What change fits?

The Scenario & Constraint

The replacement has empty caches and creates a burst of database connections. Readiness checks pass, but full cutover overwhelms a shared database. What will you change?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Verify realistic warmup and shared-dependency budgets, prewarm safe data or expose traffic gradually with guarded observation, and cap connection/concurrency growth. Test the version with representative traffic before full ownership.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Duplicating application capacity does not duplicate database capacity. Aggressive warming can itself overload the database. Combining blue-green preparation with canary exposure adds control but requires reliable routing and evidence.

Only green changes, yet blue errors too. Is rollback sufficient?

The Scenario & Constraint

The new release launches an expensive shared-database query. Control traffic on blue also slows. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Stop or contain the new workload and restore shared-dependency health, then verify both environments. Use version attribution, database evidence and resource limits to distinguish the source from its shared consequences.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Reversing traffic may leave queries or workers running. Separate full stacks improve isolation but introduce data synchronization and cost; two application environments alone do not create independent failure domains.

The old environment was deleted yesterday; can operations still promise a one-minute rollback?

The Scenario & Constraint

A delayed defect appears after the agreed retention window. Blue images remain in a registry, but its capacity and credentials are gone. What recovery promise fits?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Treat recovery as redeployment or a forward repair, check present data compatibility and measure the required provisioning steps. Define future retention and rollback windows against business recovery needs instead of leaving a perpetual fast-switch claim.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Longer retention costs capacity, patching and credential management. An archived image is not a ready environment, and an old vulnerable release may be unsafe to restore unchanged.
