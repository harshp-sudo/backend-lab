Canary Deployment

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

Before giving a new soup to every diner, serve a small, representative group and compare their experience with diners eating the old recipe at the same time. A good result from three volunteers is weak evidence, and watching the whole restaurant average can hide that every new-soup diner is unhappy.

A canary deployment limits initial production exposure and uses observations to decide whether to expand, pause or recover. It is an experiment under risk, not a magical percentage or a substitute for pre-release checks. Our example is a checkout release whose payment route behaves differently for one geography. The key questions are who receives it, which outcomes count, and how much evidence justifies the next step.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

Exposure and analysis form a loop. Missing or unrepresentative evidence is a reason to pause, not a successful verdict.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace ownership, state transitions, decision evidence and the failure/recovery boundary.

1. Declare the decision before collecting charts

Define the change, affected population, acceptable user outcomes and stop rules. Keep artifact identity stable while evaluating it; changing configuration mid-window creates a different experiment. A checkout canary should include successful business completion as well as HTTP health. Set who owns a paused deployment and how recovery is initiated; unattended ambiguity is itself operational risk.

2. Choose a coherent routing unit

A request percentage is not always a user percentage. Sticky user or tenant assignment can preserve a consistent experience and include the right data paths, but large tenants skew volume. Verify that the intended geography, payment method and request types appear. Mirror-only traffic can test read behavior yet cannot establish the correctness of real writes unless the side-effect contract is explicitly addressed.

3. Compare contemporaneous, version-specific signals

Suppose the candidate receives 5% of traffic and fails 20% of its requests while control has negligible failures. The added global failure fraction is 0.05 × 0.20 = 0.01, or 1%. The overall chart dilutes a severe candidate regression. Compare candidate and control error fractions, latency distributions and business outcomes, accounting for different traffic mixes and shared dependencies.

4. Match observation time to evidence and failure mode

A ten-minute window may contain millions of common reads yet no rare settlement operation. For a hypothetical independent defect probability p=0.01, the chance of observing at least one failure in n=100 requests is 1−0.99^100, about 63.4%; absence of failure is weak proof. This simple model assumes identical independent trials and does not establish a production confidence policy. Use required cohorts, volumes and long-lived behavior alongside elapsed time.

5. Expand in controlled stages and retain recovery

Move to a larger cohort only after explicit evaluation. Stop on harmful regression; pause on missing telemetry or insufficient evidence. Observe shared databases and queues because a candidate can harm its control through common state. A controller that shifts traffic is not necessarily able to revert a schema or external payment. Keep compatibility and safe containment ready, then record what each stage actually tested.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

Healthy average, failed cohort

Symptom: The overall chart looks normal while new users cannot pay.

Root Cause: Candidate errors are diluted by much larger control traffic.

Fix/Prevention: Slice by release and relevant bounded cohorts; inspect business success.

Timer mistaken for evidence

Symptom: A rare route breaks only after full release.

Root Cause: The canary ended on elapsed time without representative requests.

Fix/Prevention: Require coverage/volume and acknowledge unknowns; do not widen simply because the timer expired.

Control contaminated by candidate

Symptom: Both versions slow and the analyzer blames background noise.

Root Cause: They share an overloaded dependency.

Fix/Prevention: Inspect shared resources and contain candidate-induced work before interpreting the comparison.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: Choose a representative cohort, interpret attributable outcomes with enough evidence, and bound harm while uncertainty remains. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

Why does the routing unit matter?

Request, user and tenant assignments expose different populations and consistency risks. Select a unit compatible with state and representative workload.

What is wrong with only checking the global error ratio?

A small failing candidate cohort can be hidden in a healthy large control. Use version-specific denominators and related business outcomes.

Is no observed failure proof of safety?

No. Low volume, untested paths, missing telemetry and correlated events limit what the sample supports.

What can a canary controller reverse?

Only effects within its mechanism, often traffic exposure or workload selection. Persisted writes and external effects need compatible application recovery and reconciliation.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

Global success is 99%, but the candidate fails 20%. Would you promote?

The Scenario & Constraint

A 5% candidate cohort generates a 20% error fraction. Control is healthy and the global error alert has not fired. What decision fits?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Stop expansion and evaluate/reverse candidate exposure using candidate-specific user outcomes. The hypothetical additional global failure share is 1%, which explains why aggregation hides a serious candidate regression.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

An acceptable risk threshold must be explicit and workload-specific. Smaller exposure limits harm but produces fewer observations; a quiet global pager cannot replace a release decision gate.

No errors in 100 requests; the risky settlement path runs once daily. What now?

The Scenario & Constraint

The timer finishes before any settlement request reaches the candidate. The feature changes settlement rounding. What will you require?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Pause promotion for missing representative evidence, add safe targeted validation and include the affected operation/cohort in the evaluation window. Define how much remaining risk is acceptable with the owner rather than silently calling the timer a pass.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Longer live trials cost time and exposure; synthetic requests may miss real data. The simple independent-trial probability calculation is illustrative and does not cover correlated financial workflows.

Candidate latency looks better because it serves only small tenants. How do you compare?

The Scenario & Constraint

Control serves large tenants with expensive queries while candidate routing selects new small accounts. What analysis and placement changes fit?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Use a representative assignment and compare bounded like-for-like workload slices alongside user outcomes. Check sample sizes, resource placement and configuration before attributing differences to the new code.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Perfect matching may be impractical and can increase complexity. A faster average under an easier workload is not evidence of a faster implementation; broadening safely still requires a risk bound.

The candidate stops emitting metrics after deployment. Is that a pass?

The Scenario & Constraint

The analyzer sees zero recorded errors because its candidate series disappears. Traffic continues reaching the release. What do you do?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Treat missing evidence as an unknown/failing gate, check scrape/instrumentation and routing identities, and pause or contain exposure. Require usable denominators and signal freshness before resuming evaluation.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Assuming zero is dangerous; treating every monitoring failure as an immediate full rollback can also create instability. Make the unknown-state policy explicit and verify recovery observability independently.

Control and candidate both degrade after a new query ships. Which version caused it?

The Scenario & Constraint

A 2% candidate saturates a shared database with expensive work; both cohorts show similar bad latency. What action and inference are justified?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Contain candidate work, inspect query/resource evidence and observe dependency recovery. Recognize that equal cohort outcomes do not exonerate a release when they share the affected bottleneck.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

An isolated database makes attribution easier but may not reproduce real sharing and costs more. Rolling traffic back may not stop background queries; cancel or bound the causative work safely.

A five-minute canary passes, but memory exhaustion appears after six hours. What changes?

The Scenario & Constraint

The release retains per-customer state slowly, so short windows never reach the dangerous heap size. What evaluation would you add?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Add duration-appropriate soak evidence and trend/resource analysis for the state lifetime, alongside targeted tests and bounded production exposure. Keep a recovery plan and compare growth under comparable workload rather than only current latency.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Long canaries slow delivery and may consume capacity; overly noisy resource gates cause false stops. Short high-volume tests do not necessarily simulate hours of retained-state behavior.
