Circuit Breaker Pattern

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

A teacher stops sending children to a water fountain after several attempts find it broken. Later, one helper checks whether it works before the whole class returns. This avoids a growing crowd at the same broken tap. The teacher still needs rules for how long each check may take and where children can wait.

A circuit breaker changes admission to a dependency based on observed outcomes. Closed normally permits calls; open rejects/short-circuits them; half-open allows a controlled test of recovery. It handles sustained trouble differently from simply retrying every request.

Use a checkout page with a required payment action and optional recommendations. Their fallback and breaker scopes should differ. A breaker is not itself a timeout, concurrency limit or guarantee that the downstream service has recovered. Its thresholds and states describe local policy; they do not automatically represent global truth.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

Thresholds, windows and transition details belong to the chosen implementation/configuration. A probe needs its own timeout and concurrency budget.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace the actual actor, communication, acknowledgment and recovery boundary.

1. Define the protected call and meaningful outcome

Choose dependency/operation scope and classify errors. A malformed user request may be a definite business rejection, while connection failures or excessive latency can indicate a degraded dependency. A successful transport response can still contain a failed business operation. Decide what the breaker observes without hiding important failures.

Do not combine payment and recommendations into one undifferentiated health bucket. Isolate the scope enough that an optional feature cannot disable an unrelated critical operation, while avoiding unbounded per-user breaker objects that consume memory.

2. Choose evidence and thresholds with a minimum sample

A breaker may use counts or time windows, error rates, slow-call rates and a minimum number of calls. With tiny samples, one failure can otherwise create excessive trips. Windows and thresholds trade detection speed against false positives; tune them using the operation’s real demand and failure behavior.

Resilience4j’s guide documents sliding windows and minimum-call/half-open controls, but this lesson supplies no unpinned Java API configuration. Treat any illustrative threshold as a workload decision, not a universally correct library default. Record the actual version/configuration before deployment.

3. Keep waiting and concurrency bounded separately

Calls admitted while closed can all still be in flight. If the dependency becomes slow, thousands may block before enough completed outcomes cause a trip. A slow-call threshold typically observes duration; it does not necessarily interrupt a running call at that duration.

Use explicit connect/request/total deadlines, bounded dependency concurrency and queues. Resilience4j documents that a CircuitBreaker alone does not restrict concurrent calls. A bulkhead and appropriate cancellation/release behavior address that different resource problem. Verify wrapper ordering because it changes which events the breaker sees.

4. Make open-state responses honest

While open, skip the protected attempt and provide a permitted cached/degraded result or a clear error. For recommendations, a generic safe list may be useful. For payment, returning a made-up success would corrupt the business workflow; expose unavailable/pending according to actual state.

Avoid a fallback that synchronously overloads another fragile dependency. Observe fallback failures separately and preserve authorization/freshness boundaries on cached data. A fallback is another implementation path with its own capacity and correctness assumptions.

5. Probe and recover without a new stampede

Half-open should allow a bounded number of probes while other callers follow the open policy or another explicitly bounded admission rule. Probe completion and configured evidence decide whether to reopen or close. Merely waiting a cooldown does not prove the dependency is healthy.

With many caller instances, each local breaker may probe at about the same time. Apply suitable staggering and fleet-level admission limits where needed, then ramp useful traffic. Per-instance state avoids a shared coordination dependency but can produce different local views; that is a design trade-off, not automatically a bug.

6. Observe state transitions and compose retries deliberately

Measure calls permitted, rejected, timed out, failed and successful, along with state changes, probe outcomes and fallback use. Test low volume, partial target failure, latency spikes and recovery. Make reset/override procedures operationally clear without letting a forced close flood a sick dependency.

Retries can be inside or outside a breaker wrapper and can multiply observed attempts or fail-fast rejections differently. Assign retry ownership, preserve the total deadline and avoid retrying immediately against an open circuit. Test the concrete composition, not just each pattern in isolation.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

Threads exhaust before breaker opens

Symptom: Many slow calls block despite a breaker.

Root Cause: Admission was not concurrency-bounded and running calls lacked deadlines.

Fix/Prevention: Combine the breaker with appropriate bulkheads, timeouts and resource release.

A failed payment looks successful

Symptom: Fallback returns an ordinary success payload.

Root Cause: Availability metrics displaced the real business contract.

Fix/Prevention: Return honest pending/unavailable state and reconcile uncertain effects.

Recovery crashes the dependency again

Symptom: All instances probe or resume full traffic together.

Root Cause: Cooldown expiration was treated as evidence of unlimited capacity.

Fix/Prevention: Bound/stagger probes and test the aggregate recovery ramp.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: A breaker controls which attempts are admitted from failure evidence; safe behavior also needs honest fallbacks, bounded resources, deadlines and measured recovery. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

What is open meant to do?

Short-circuit protected attempts while the policy considers them unlikely to help, using an honest fallback or error.

Why have half-open?

To gather bounded recovery evidence before allowing normal traffic again.

Does a breaker limit concurrent in-flight calls?

Not by itself; explicit concurrency limits, queues, deadlines and cancellation address resource occupancy.

Why classify errors and scope?

Different operations/outcomes represent different failure evidence and must not disable unrelated useful work.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

The breaker is closed while all worker threads block. What do you add?

The Scenario & Constraint

Dependency latency rose, but calls have not yet completed to contribute failure evidence. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Set actual request deadlines and bounded dependency concurrency/queue admission, then tune slow/failure observation for the workload. Verify timed-out work releases resources or remains accounted for until it ends.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Limits reject or defer work, but protect unrelated requests. Lowering an error threshold alone cannot reclaim already occupied threads or terminate an arbitrary client call.

Bad user input trips the payment breaker. What changes?

The Scenario & Constraint

Invalid-card/request errors dominate while the provider is otherwise healthy. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Classify definite business/client rejections separately from dependency transport/availability failures, preserve useful metrics for both, and configure the breaker against evidence of the protected failure mode.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Ignoring the wrong error can hide a real provider-wide incident. Revisit classification when provider semantics change; do not assume every HTTP 4xx or 5xx has one universal meaning.

One failure disables a rarely used operation for minutes. How do you tune it?

The Scenario & Constraint

The breaker evaluates a tiny sample and false trips block valid work. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Choose a minimum sample/window and recovery policy appropriate to the operation, considering consecutive failures and latency evidence under the library’s supported controls. Test low-rate histories.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Waiting for evidence can delay detection. A rare high-risk operation may prefer explicit fail-closed behavior, but that is a deliberate contract rather than copying a high-volume percentage threshold.

Hundreds of callers half-open together. What will you do?

The Scenario & Constraint

Each instance permits several probes and their combined load overwhelms the recovering service. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Bound/stagger local probes and apply an aggregate dependency-capacity strategy where needed; ramp recovered traffic with observability instead of assuming one local probe limit bounds the fleet.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Coordination can add a dependency and local staggering cannot guarantee a global cap alone. A shared admission point may fit some deployments, but must have its own failure plan.

The fallback says payment succeeded. Is checkout resilient?

The Scenario & Constraint

The actual charge outcome is unavailable or unknown. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Return an honest unavailable/pending business state and reconcile via stable payment intent/status semantics. Use fallbacks only for results whose reduced correctness/freshness contract is acceptable.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Checkout conversion may fall temporarily, but fabricated payment success is not valid recovery. Recommendations can have a weaker fallback because their business consequences differ.

Retries keep firing while the circuit is open. What do you inspect?

The Scenario & Constraint

Several middleware layers each add retries and count failures differently. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Map the actual wrapper/call order, choose one bounded retry owner, preserve the end-to-end deadline and prevent immediate repeated attempts for known short-circuit rejection. Verify which outcomes each metric counts.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Changing composition changes observed rates and user latency. A retry around a whole workflow can repeat earlier effects, so stable intent and operation semantics remain necessary.
