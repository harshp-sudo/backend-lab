Distributed Systems & Scalability

A town grows from one busy shop to many shops connected by roads and phones. More shops add capacity, but broken phones, delayed deliveries and competing instructions create new problems. Distributed systems design decides how the town keeps useful promises even when its parts disagree or cannot communicate.

Follow four decisions: how to grow capacity, what a read or successful command means, who coordinates shared work, and how failures are contained. Distribution is useful only when the extra capacity or resilience justifies its communication and recovery costs.

Suggested reading guide

Begin with Scaling & Traffic Distribution to locate the bottleneck and distinguish adding capacity, directing requests and assigning keys.

Continue to Consistency Models & Trade-Offs for partition-time decisions, PACELC and precise reader-visible guarantees. The new CAP lesson links to the existing database treatment.
