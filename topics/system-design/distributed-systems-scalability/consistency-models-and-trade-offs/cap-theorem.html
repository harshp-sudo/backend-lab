CAP Theorem

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

Two playground supervisors share a red stop sign by phone. Their phone line breaks. One learns the playground must close, but the other cannot hear that instruction. The isolated supervisor can keep using the old instruction or stop admitting children until the instruction is trustworthy. A faster phone cannot deliver a message across a line that is still broken.

This lesson focuses on distributed network partitions, operation-specific CP/AP decisions and PACELC. The existing database CAP lesson develops the database-oriented model and remains a separate learning item. Here the running example is a regional configuration service whose clients distinguish a safety-critical disable command from a noncritical display preference.

CAP is an impossibility result under a specified model, not a three-feature shopping list. Its consistency is an atomic/linearizable object contract, not ACID invariant validity. Its availability requires the modeled valid response from non-failing participants; an arbitrary error or endless wait is not a successful escape. Real service-level latency and uptime targets need separate definitions.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

Apply the choices to an explicit operation and allowed failure. PACELC also asks what replication costs when the network is functioning; it does not turn every product into a permanent two-letter label.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace the actual actor, communication, acknowledgment and recovery boundary.

1. State the operation before assigning letters

For a display preference, the service may promise that replicas eventually converge while an isolated region returns an older valid preference. For a global disable flag, the product may require that no read beginning after an acknowledged disable returns enabled. These are different contracts even if their keys share infrastructure.

Write the participants, read/write semantics and what acknowledgment means. An API that honestly returns “unknown, do not proceed” can protect users, but it gives up the original always-completing read contract where the theorem requires a value. Business-safe degradation and CAP availability are different measurements.

2. Trace two indistinguishable histories

Suppose region B is cut off. In history one, A receives and completes a disable; in history two, nobody changes the flag. B receives the same later read and no message from A in either history. It cannot infer which remote event happened merely from its local state.

If B must always return its old enabled value, there is an allowed history that violates the current-value guarantee. If it refuses or waits for missing information, it sacrifices availability for that operation. A failure detector’s timeout is suspicion of communication trouble, not proof of the absent remote history.

3. Explain CP/AP as explicit partition behavior

A CP-oriented operation preserves the selected consistency promise and restricts requests that cannot establish authority. A majority partition may still serve useful work; refusing the minority means the full availability property at every non-failing participant is not maintained.

An AP-oriented operation retains the required local responses under the model but needs a weaker value/conflict contract. “AP” does not mean every answer is correct for every business invariant, and “CP” does not describe a latency SLO or all endpoints in a vendor product. State topology, configuration and operation when applying a label.

4. Add PACELC for healthy-network reasoning

PACELC asks: during a partition, how are availability and consistency traded; else, how are latency and consistency traded in the replicated design? Even without failure, waiting for remote coordination may conflict with a very tight local response objective. The question exposes this normal-operation cost.

For the configuration example, a noncritical display read could tolerate delayed replication and stay local. A global disable acknowledgment may require supported coordination. Name the guaranteed read contract, actual geographic path and measured latency rather than claiming a classification alone predicts milliseconds. Historical product classifications are not current configuration guarantees.

5. Choose a supported protocol and separate its surfaces

Use a system whose documented operation semantics match the requirement. For example, etcd v3.6 documents default linearizable KV operations and a weaker serializable read option; watches have separate guarantees. A watch-maintained local copy is not automatically a linearizable read at the moment a user acts.

Quorum counts alone are not a protocol proof. Concurrent operations, version selection, write completion, membership and recovery matter. Likewise, synchronous replication is not a complete definition of linearizability, and changing a client read option can change the visible contract without changing the product name.

6. Plan repair and the cost of promises already exposed

After reconnection, reconcile divergent preferences or resynchronize obsolete readers before announcing recovery. For a safety-critical command, inspect whether any client acted on an old instruction and whether a bounded permission/lease protocol or fail-closed behavior was required.

Converging configuration cannot reverse an action already performed. Track observed versions, stale-read age, rejected/pending operations and repair backlog. Test asymmetric isolation as well as a clean two-way split. Keep the documented database CAP link as a complementary treatment, not a substitute for this operation-level reasoning.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

A stop flag is only eventually enforced

Symptom: An isolated service keeps executing after global disable acknowledgment.

Root Cause: A local watched cache was advertised as a current authorization decision.

Fix/Prevention: Specify propagation/lease bounds or coordinate/fail closed at the relevant action boundary.

Fast errors counted as available reads

Symptom: A design says errors satisfy every CAP requirement.

Root Cause: Transport completion was confused with a valid modeled value.

Fix/Prevention: Define the object response and name the availability concession honestly.

Product label replaces design

Symptom: Two endpoints with different read modes receive one universal CP/AP label.

Root Cause: Configuration and operation scope were omitted.

Fix/Prevention: Verify actual read/write/watch semantics for the chosen version and failure.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: Specify one operation, trace missing information during partitions, use PACELC for normal replication costs, and defend exactly which user-visible guarantee changes. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

Why cannot an isolated region always infer the latest remote update?

Executions with and without that update can look identical locally while communication is unavailable.

Does an unavailable error preserve CAP availability?

Not when the modeled operation requires a valid successful object response; the theorem is not merely about returning bytes.

What extra question does PACELC ask?

What latency/consistency choice the replicated system makes during ordinary operation without a partition.

Why inspect a watch separately from a KV read?

Different APIs can promise different ordering/freshness; a derived watched copy does not inherit every read guarantee.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

An isolated region misses a global kill switch. What promise can you keep?

The Scenario & Constraint

The product wants every action after acknowledged disable to be rejected, even in a region that cannot communicate. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Require the action to establish supported current authority or use an explicitly bounded authorization scheme whose promise is different. Fail closed/pending where that authority is unavailable; state which operations lose availability.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

This can stop safe work too. Allowing an old cached permission changes the requirement; a short refresh interval is not a proof of immediate global revocation. If bounded delay is acceptable, expose and verify that bound.

May a profile-color service remain writable in both isolated regions?

The Scenario & Constraint

Color changes are reversible and the product accepts a documented conflict rule and delayed convergence. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Permit local updates under that weaker contract, retain enough version/conflict information and define reconciliation plus user-visible behavior. Test concurrent edits and reconnection.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Users can see older or overridden preferences. Routing every write to one authoritative region is a simpler alternative if partition-time local writes are unnecessary; do not transfer the color policy to a uniqueness invariant.

There is no partition. Why can current global reads still be slow?

The Scenario & Constraint

The service promises a real-time global read contract while users are far from coordination participants. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Trace the actual coordination path and measure its normal latency. Use PACELC to explain the guarantee/latency choice, then consider regional ownership, a weaker permitted read or a revised SLO.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Reducing coordination can change the read promise. Adding nodes does not remove geographic communication delay, and historical product labels cannot determine the current deployment’s latency.

Does R plus W greater than N settle the design?

The Scenario & Constraint

A proposal claims linearizable failover solely because read and write quorums overlap. Concurrent writers and membership changes are unspecified. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Require the actual protocol and its documented guarantee, including version ordering, concurrent reads/writes, recovery and configuration changes. Trace a complete operation history rather than approving the arithmetic alone.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Protocol verification costs more effort than a slogan. A supported coordination system may simplify it, but its weaker optional read surfaces must still be chosen correctly.

Can preallocating identifiers evade the theorem?

The Scenario & Constraint

Each disconnected region receives a disjoint finite ID range and only allocates within that range. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Use the scoped authority if it meets the product requirement, with safe durable range assignment, exhaustion handling and transfer rules. Explain that the independent allocation object differs from an always-current global register.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

A region can exhaust its rights while another has spare capacity. Rebalancing those rights may require coordination; arbitrary disconnected updates to one global object do not become free.

Replicas agree after healing. Was every earlier action safe?

The Scenario & Constraint

Two isolated services acted on conflicting policy versions and reconciliation now picks one final value. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Audit the externally exposed decisions separately, repair or compensate where possible, and redesign the critical action boundary if the original invariant was violated. Convergence answers a state question, not whether all earlier promises were valid.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Compensation may be costly or impossible. If the requirement only concerns future display convergence, weaker semantics may be acceptable; an irreversible action needs its own authority/verification contract.
