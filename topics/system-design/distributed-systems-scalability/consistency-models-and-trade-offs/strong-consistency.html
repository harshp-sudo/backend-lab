Strong Consistency

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

A teacher keeps one official sign-out sheet for a classroom camera. If a child finishes signing it out before another child checks, the second child must see it as unavailable. Several helpers can hold copies, but they cannot promise to behave like that one sheet merely because they all have pencils.

Strong consistency is an umbrella phrase; name the actual guarantee before designing around it. This lesson uses linearizability for operations on a register or atomic conditional-update object: each completed operation appears to take effect at one instant between invocation and response, respecting real-time precedence.

Use an equipment reservation record as a worked example. A read beginning after an acknowledged reservation must not return an older unreserved state under a linearizable object contract. Concurrent operations may be ordered either way where the object specification permits. Multi-object transaction isolation, durability and permission enforcement are additional questions, not synonyms for this property.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

The ordering requirement comes from completion before invocation. If the read overlaps another write, the valid result depends on where the operations can be placed in a legal object history.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace the actual actor, communication, acknowledgment and recovery boundary.

1. Specify the object and legal operations

A register supports reads and writes of a value; a reservation object may expose reserve-if-free as one indivisible conditional change. Define successful, rejected and unknown outcomes. The correctness question is whether the observed history can be explained by a legal sequential execution with each operation inside its real invocation/response interval.

A read is not required to predict a write that completes after the read returns. Nor must it necessarily include a concurrent write that finishes while it is in progress. “Latest” without an interval and object specification is ambiguous; draw the operation timeline.

2. Separate real-time ordering from transaction isolation

Linearizability concerns the apparent atomic behavior of specified operations. Serializability asks whether transactions are equivalent to a sequential transaction ordering; ordinary serializability alone need not enforce real-time precedence. Strict serializability combines transaction serialization with real-time requirements.

Even two individually linearizable reads and writes do not turn a read-then-write client sequence into an atomic reservation. If two clients both observe free, each may later write reserved. Use a supported atomic conditional update or a transaction with the required invariant/isolation rather than strengthening the adjective on separate reads.

3. Find the actual authority and acknowledgment point

A replicated implementation needs a supported protocol that determines when a change is accepted and how a reader obtains a valid observation. Do not invent a strong guarantee from synchronous copying, a leader label or overlapping quorum counts alone. Membership, failure recovery and concurrent requests are part of the algorithm.

Write down what success means if the leader fails immediately afterward. Durability concerns retained state under the documented failure model; ordering concerns allowed histories. A system can lose data outside its promised failure tolerance without that fact being explained solely by the word consistency.

4. Choose the read surface deliberately

etcd v3.6 documents linearizable KV operations by default, while its serializable read option can serve stale data and watch APIs have separate guarantees. Its use of the word serializable for a read mode must not be confused with a generic SQL transaction isolation tutorial.

A cache or local watch subscriber is a derived observation unless an additional protocol makes the action meet the required contract. For a reservation display a stale view may be fine; the final reserve-if-free action must enforce authority at the actual decision boundary. Rechecking once and later acting independently leaves another race.

5. Handle communication loss and unknown results

During a partition, a participant that cannot establish the promised authority may need to wait or fail. Bound user waiting with deadlines and expose uncertainty honestly. A timed-out write might have taken effect; blindly repeating a non-idempotent action can create a different problem even if each attempt is linearizable.

Use operation identity or a status/reconciliation path for retryable commands. Test the sequence “write committed, reply lost, client retries” separately from a rejected write. Strong observation guarantees do not automatically create exactly-once external effects.

6. Validate histories and scope in production

Collect operation IDs, invocation/completion intervals, versions/results and failure events. Check invariants and representative concurrent histories against the documented operation specification; a finite test can find violations, not prove every future execution correct. Use established implementations and their guarantee documentation.

Measure latency and availability costs across regions. Restrict stronger coordination to operations that need it while explicitly labeling weaker views. Test minority isolation, leader change, stale cached state and simultaneous reserve attempts. A faster path is acceptable only if its actual promise fits the business action.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

Read then write oversells

Symptom: Two clients reserve one camera after separate successful reads.

Root Cause: The whole decision was never one atomic operation.

Fix/Prevention: Use conditional mutation or an appropriate transaction at the invariant owner.

A watch is advertised as a current read

Symptom: Local policy state trails a completed update.

Root Cause: An event stream and cached snapshot were given stronger semantics than documented.

Fix/Prevention: Use the correct read/action API or a specified version-aware protocol.

Strong means durable forever

Symptom: An acknowledged change is assumed safe under every disaster.

Root Cause: Ordering and failure-retention promises were conflated.

Fix/Prevention: Document durability, replication, backups and tolerated failures separately.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: Name the exact operation guarantee and enforce the entire business decision at the matching atomic boundary; strong observations alone do not make a workflow atomic or duplicate-safe. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

Where does a linearized operation appear to take effect?

At some point between its invocation and response, inside a legal history respecting completed-before-started precedence.

Must a read include every write completing before the read returns?

Not necessarily if the write overlaps the read; the entire history must admit a legal ordering within the operation intervals.

Why can linearizable reads and writes still permit a reservation race?

Two separate operations do not make the intervening predicate-and-update indivisible.

What else must be specified besides strong consistency?

The precise object/API guarantee, transaction scope, durability/failure assumptions, and command retry/external-effect semantics.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

Two current reads both return free. Can both reservations succeed?

The Scenario & Constraint

Clients perform separate read and unconditional write calls against a linearizable register. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Expose reserve-if-free as an atomic conditional operation at the authoritative object or use the required transaction boundary; handle a failed condition explicitly.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Contention can reject callers and require a new decision. Faster current reads do not make the pair atomic; if reservations span several resources, a larger transaction/workflow decision is needed.

A read returns version 8 while concurrent write 9 finishes. Is that a violation?

The Scenario & Constraint

The read started before write 9 and returned afterward; version 8 was the prior valid state. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Inspect the full history and whether the read can legally be placed before the overlapping write. Do not classify it as stale solely because write 9 completed before the read response.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

A stricter product requirement about response-time freshness would need a separately stated contract. A read starting after write 9 completes cannot be ordered before that completed write.

Can a cached current permission authorize an irreversible action?

The Scenario & Constraint

The cache is refreshed by a watch but revocation must affect every action after acknowledgment. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Enforce authorization at a boundary with the required current-state/atomic-action semantics, or explicitly revise to a bounded permission protocol. Treat the local watch copy as a separate observation surface.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Synchronous authority checks can reduce availability and add latency. A check followed by an unrelated later action can still race with revocation; choose a scope that matches the promised effect.

A minority replica is reachable but cannot contact authority. Serve anyway?

The Scenario & Constraint

Clients require the same linearizable object contract during a network split. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Use the supported protocol’s valid read/commit path and reject or wait within a deadline when it cannot establish the guarantee. State the sacrificed availability rather than silently serving an old copy.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

A weaker explicitly labeled display read may remain useful. It must not be presented as an authoritative command result merely to improve an availability counter.

A strong write times out. Was it not applied?

The Scenario & Constraint

The connection breaks after the service may have committed the equipment reservation. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Treat the outcome as unknown and query/retry through a stable operation identity and supported status semantics, preserving the original intent rather than issuing a new independent reservation.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Operation history retention costs space and needs an expiry policy. Strong ordering does not identify duplicate intent; if the original API has no reconciliation mechanism, expose uncertainty honestly.

Two individually strong records violate a joint budget. What changes?

The Scenario & Constraint

The invariant limits combined reservations across records A and B; callers update them independently. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Move the invariant to one supported atomic owner/conditional operation or use a transaction/workflow whose guarantees actually cover both records. Define partial failure and concurrent requests.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

A common owner can bottleneck and a broader transaction costs coordination. If the business can allocate independent rights, a decomposed model may avoid global coordination with different operational constraints.
