Two-Phase Commit (2PC)

🎯 The Hook & ELI5

Two teachers must both approve a trip before anyone confirms it. First each says, “I have reserved what you need and will wait for your final instruction.” Then the organizer records one decision and tells both teachers. If the organizer disappears after they promise to wait, the teachers cannot safely invent opposite answers.

Two-phase commit coordinates an atomic commit/abort outcome among transactional participants. In phase one, participants prepare: a yes vote is a durable promise to follow the eventual decision. In phase two, the coordinator records and communicates the decision. Classic 2PC can block prepared participants when the decision cannot be established.

Use two internal inventory databases as participants in one allocation. This is not a protocol for arbitrary HTTP services without prepare/recovery support. It also is not two-phase locking, which concerns concurrency control. A complete distributed database may combine atomic commit, isolation and replicated recovery, but their roles must remain distinct.

🗺️ High-Level Big Picture

A participant’s prepared yes is not final commit. The durable coordinator decision must survive a lost message or process restart; successful application acknowledgments are another step.

🔍 Step-by-Step Mechanics

Follow the worked example one decision at a time. Open each step to connect the everyday model to the implementation; examples state their product/runtime scope explicitly. Trace the actual actor, communication, acknowledgment and recovery boundary.

1. Establish identity and participant scope

Give the transaction a stable identity and determine which resources participate under the transaction manager’s protocol. Each participant executes its local work under the required isolation/concurrency control. Include timeouts and ownership of recovery in the supported implementation rather than relying on a process-local list.

For the allocation example, a row update in each database is included only if that participant can prepare and later recover its decision. A message to an unrelated shipping service is not included merely because it was sent before the coordinator’s final log entry.

2. Prepare means a recoverable promise

A participant that votes yes must make enough information durable to honor commit or abort after a crash, while retaining whatever resources the implementation requires. A participant unable to prepare votes no/aborts according to the protocol. The coordinator cannot commit if a required participant did not provide the necessary yes.

Prepared is an in-doubt state, not a third final outcome. Holding locks or versions while prepared can affect unrelated transactions and maintenance. The exact resources and persistence format depend on the database; no client-side memory flag can substitute for durable participant support.

3. Record one authoritative decision before dissemination

After all required yes votes, the coordinator durably records commit before sending commit instructions. A no vote or defined failure path can lead to abort. Recovery uses the stable protocol state so a restarted coordinator does not choose a fresh conflicting outcome for the same transaction.

Messages can be delayed, duplicated or lost. Participants must identify the transaction and handle repeated final decisions consistently. Different participants can learn the decision at different times; atomic outcome is not a claim that every independent outside read sees all effects at one wall-clock instant.

4. Distinguish timeout states

Before a participant has promised prepared, the protocol may permit local abort. After it has voted yes and lost the decision path, a timeout alone does not prove abort is safe: another participant may already have committed under a recorded decision. The implementation must establish a valid decision or remain prepared.

This waiting is classic 2PC blocking. Coordinator replication or more advanced commit protocols can improve tolerance under specified assumptions, but “uses consensus somewhere” is not a complete nonblocking atomic-commit argument. Quorum progress, participant availability and failure timing still matter.

5. Use actual participant facilities deliberately

PostgreSQL 18 provides PREPARE TRANSACTION, COMMIT PREPARED and ROLLBACK PREPARED for external transaction-manager integration. Prepared work can outlive a session and appears in pg_prepared_xacts. The feature needs deliberate configuration and operational management; long-lived prepared transactions can retain locks and interfere with cleanup.

Inspecting the view can help identify old entries, but age alone does not reveal the authoritative outcome. The named PostgreSQL facilities are documentation examples here, not an executed coordinator or permission to unilaterally finish an unknown transaction. Only act from verified transaction-manager state.

6. Recover and monitor before the incident

Keep durable coordinator/participant recovery information under supported retention rules. Track prepared age/count, resource pressure, decision-delivery backlog and recovery errors. Alert before prepared work fills operational limits, and rehearse coordinator restart plus participant loss at each phase.

A recovery procedure must establish the existing decision, apply it idempotently and preserve auditability. If the required evidence is destroyed, manual resolution can involve a genuine consistency decision, not a harmless timeout cleanup. Backups and disaster recovery must cover transaction-manager state as well as the participants.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls, Bugs & What-If Scenarios

Read each failure as a story: diagnose the visible symptom, identify its cause, and change the mechanism that produced it.

Timeout rollback splits the outcome

Symptom: One participant aborts after another committed.

Root Cause: Prepared uncertainty was mistaken for proof of abort.

Fix/Prevention: Recover the authoritative decision through the supported manager.

Coordinator forgets after restart

Symptom: Participants wait while a new manager starts a new transaction.

Root Cause: Decision/identity recovery existed only in memory.

Fix/Prevention: Persist manager state and recover the same transaction identity.

Prepared entries grow without bound

Symptom: Locks remain and maintenance stalls.

Root Cause: Prepare was used without timely completion monitoring.

Fix/Prevention: Set operational alerts and recovery ownership; do not blindly abort by age.

🧠 Active Recall & Mastery Check

Summarize this in your own words: Prepared means durable uncertainty until a valid final decision is established; protect recovery evidence and never replace that decision with an arbitrary timeout. Explain a concrete example, name an assumption that changes the answer, and sketch the relevant diagram without looking.

What does a yes vote promise?

The participant can recover and follow the protocol’s final decision, rather than simply forgetting its local work.

Why record commit before broadcasting it?

After any participant acts, coordinator recovery must recover that same decision rather than invent another.

Why can a prepared participant wait indefinitely?

If it cannot establish the final decision, independently choosing abort could conflict with a participant already committed.

How is 2PC different from two-phase locking?

2PC coordinates commit outcome across participants; 2PL is a concurrency-control discipline. A system may use both for different purposes.

Tricky & Most-Asked Interview Scenarios

All participants prepared and the coordinator crashed. Abort after thirty seconds?

The Scenario & Constraint

No verified final decision is locally available. Another participant might have received commit. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Recover/contact the supported decision authority or use the protocol’s legitimate termination/recovery procedure; keep in-doubt participants prepared when safety cannot be established. Escalate operational pressure without guessing.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Waiting reduces availability and retains resources. A supported replicated manager can reduce this failure exposure, but elapsed time alone is not evidence of a global abort.

One participant commits and the other misses the message. Is the transaction restarted?

The Scenario & Constraint

The coordinator’s commit record survives and participant B remains prepared. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Redeliver the recorded commit for the same transaction identity and let B finish through the supported recovery path. Repeated delivery must not create a second allocation.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Decision retention and retry capacity cost resources. Restarting the business operation with a new identity could duplicate A’s effect; recovery completes the old outcome.

One required participant votes no. Can the others commit?

The Scenario & Constraint

Two resources are required for one atomic allocation and one cannot prepare its change. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Choose/record abort under the transaction-manager protocol and direct prepared participants to abort. Report the original operation as unsuccessful or retryable under its documented identity/contract.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

A different business workflow could permit a partial reservation, but that changes the promise. Ignoring the no vote is not an optimization of atomic commit.

The coordinator restarts without its durable transaction log. What is safe?

The Scenario & Constraint

Prepared database entries survive, but manager state was stored only on ephemeral disk. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Treat this as recovery-state loss, stop inventing final decisions, recover authoritative logs/backups where possible and reconcile through an explicit incident procedure. Fix manager durability and restore testing.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Manual resolution may require business-level analysis and cannot promise automatic atomic recovery from evidence that was destroyed. Replicating participant data alone did not protect the coordinator decision.

Can an operator clear every old pg_prepared_xacts entry?

The Scenario & Constraint

Prepared transactions have aged during an outage and block maintenance. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Map each entry to its transaction manager, inspect the durable decision and finish it using the supported commit/abort path. Establish alerts and a recovery owner before changing operational limits.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Age-based cleanup is tempting but can contradict a committed participant. A supported protocol may define a safe presumed-abort condition, but it must be established from that protocol’s actual state and rules.

An email is sent during phase one. Will abort retract it?

The Scenario & Constraint

The mail provider has no prepare/rollback participation and the local database transaction later aborts. What will you do and why?

The Direct Answer & Architectural Decision

Keep the irreversible notification outside the claimed atomic participant set and publish it from a durable post-decision workflow/outbox with duplicate-safe delivery semantics where available.

Key Trade-Offs & Follow-Up Traps

Notification delay is a cost; compensation may send a correction but cannot make the original message never arrive. Database prepare does not automatically enlist an HTTP side effect.
